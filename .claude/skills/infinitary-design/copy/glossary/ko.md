# Korean (`ko`)

The reader is a Korean-speaking person who runs their own projects with an AI crew and has never opened a terminal.
Sentences use 해요체, polite and warm without the stiffness of 합니다체, and labels, buttons and menu items are plain noun phrases (e.g. "설정 열기", "병합됨").
The person is addressed as "선장님" when the first mate speaks to them, and "선장" alone in feed labels such as "선장 · …"; "your" is usually left out, as Korean does, and the person's own voice is "나" / "내".
Korean uses Western punctuation: a period, comma, question mark and colon with no space before them, and "·" and "→" kept as in English.
A number sits directly against its counter or unit with no space ("3개", "5분", "{count}줄"), but a measurement unit written in Latin letters keeps a space ("1.6 GB", "{kilobytes} KB").
Particles after a filled value are avoided by word order, or written in the paired form "을(를)", "은(는)", "이(가)" where they cannot be avoided; "에", "에서" and "의" need no pair.
